The problem

Out of the squat — then what?

Someone who has just obtained papers receives an integration income (RIS) for a single person: €1,340/month. A Brussels studio costs €700–850 in rent, plus a deposit. That leaves €565 for everything else. No saving, never a deposit, and the first accident sends you back to the street.

Between the squat and that studio, there is nothing.

A squat already lets you live cheaply. What it doesn't give you: a secure housing title — so an address, so a registered residence, so access to rights — and the certainty of not being evicted before your savings are done.

At the end of 2025, 62,234 households are waiting for social housing in Brussels — about 10% of all Brussels households — with waits of 9 to 13 years depending on the type. Between 2020 and 2024 the Region invested around €2 billion, built over 2,000 homes and renovated over 7,500 — and demand still rose 25% over the same period.

What we're building

50 rooms, €300 a month, all included.

50 private rooms of at least 18 m² net, each with its own shower and toilet. An older Brussels building of about 1,200 m², held under a long lease (emphyteusis, up to 99 years) from a public or institutional owner.

No purchase: at €300/month the model carries no acquisition. Since the 2021 reform of the Civil Code, a free emphyteutic lease for non-profit purposes, granted to a non-profit ASBL, is explicitly allowed. The ground rent can be zero.

A private shower and toilet is not comfort. It secures "single person" status at the CPAS — €447/month more income per resident — which is what makes €300/month payable. This line is never cut.

The repayment

Why it's not a donation.

Legal form: a subordinated loan at 0%, no repayment in the first five years, repayment in one amount between year 6 and year 10. Subordinated means: in a crisis, the bank is paid before the citizens. That is exactly what lets the bank treat the €120,000 as quasi-equity and keep the leverage at €900,000.

The cycle takes 6 to 10 years, not 5. We'd rather say it now than discover it together.

If the bank refuses subordination, the €120,000 becomes cooperative shares — equity with no exit. Leverage and budget stay the same, but the money doesn't come back.

Objections

Four objections, answered straight.

Who decides — the lenders?

The residents decide. Lenders bring the capital; residents hold the majority of votes in the general assembly and half the seats on the board. One person, one vote.

Why not a social rental agency, social housing, or a CLTB project?

Social housing counts 62,234 applicants at the end of 2025, with 9–13 year waits. The CLTB builds ownership for households, with over 1,300 people on its waiting list. Others create community housing with building sponsorship — Fair Ground Brussels, L'Îlot, Renovassistance.

What nobody offers today: an airlock of three to four years, at €300 all included, in a private room with "single person" status, for people coming out of squats. That combination is what's missing — not social housing.

50 places is little, and the first key comes in three years.

50 places with a three-to-four-year stay means 250 to 350 people over twenty years — from a single building, and the citizen capital moves on to a second one. Nothing is suspended meanwhile: the occupations continue, the support work continues.

And if it fails?

Entry costs are capped at €30,000, mostly grant-fundable, and the project stops cleanly at four defined points before a single citizen euro moves.
